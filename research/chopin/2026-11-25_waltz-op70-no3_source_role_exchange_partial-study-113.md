## Musical principle extracted, with attribution discipline
The NIFC and PTNA descriptions jointly motivate a **role-exchange model**: an upper melody plus independent inner strand in the opening, and a prominent bass melody in a contrasting section. This is evidence for investigating *foreground voice transfer* in a 3/4 waltz, NOT evidence for any uninspected note, precise bar, or contrapuntal interval. The G-flat section and A-B-C-A-B plan are PTNA secondary assertions pending first-edition score checking. NIFC describes texture with thirds/fifths/sixths, but those intervals have not been counted in the source. No source-verified specific pitch or measure is asserted.

## Original generative design — not a Chopin transcription
Define PhrasePlan(section, witness, cadence_goal, foreground_voice, countervoice, bass_role, harmonic_rhythm, anchor_status). At section boundaries, permit foreground voice to move from upper to lower register while upper/inner voices become accompanimental or contrapuntal; keep independently directed lines and an explicit bass/harmony plan. Do not equate 'bass melody' with ordinary beat-one root-only waltz bass.

A 2-bar generator first selects a 6-quarter-beat harmonic/bass trajectory, then an identifiable rhythmic-contour motive, then independent foreground and countervoice attacks. A subsequent 2-bar unit must preserve a verifiable motif invariant (contour segment, rhythmic cell, or accent placement) and transform a real event property (register, rhythm, harmonic support, contrapuntal entrance); a metadata change alone is insufficient. No requirement for mechanical 3-note cells. 16th = 1/4 quarter beat; 32nd = 1/8; use rational time and explicit rests. Cross-bar ties need both tie-out and tie-in, identical spelled pitch, and no reattack. Label dissonance as candidate only until local chord spelling, accent, approach, preparation and resolution are checked. Return AMBIGUOUS when evidence is insufficient.

Suggested synthetic counterexample: in 3/4, a low voice plays a 2-beat sustained thematic tone followed by two eighth-note steps while upper voice articulates chords; a simplistic bass-root classifier would incorrectly erase its melodic identity. This is **invented test material**, not a Chopin quotation.
